EXECUTIVE SUMMARY · 30-SECOND READ
The real argument is not "should we build AI room-type recommendations" but "should AI recommendations become the default state of the booking page". Light users beg for "decide for me" to escape information overload, while heavy users fear that "having decisions made for me" erodes platform trust. The two sides converge on 4 hard rules; disagreement centers on default form and entry placement.
⚙️ DECISIONS NEEDED
① Default-on AI placement, or persona-tiered?
② Preference handling: dynamic re-personalization or locked memory?
⚠️ TOP RISK
Heavy users perceiving "the platform is choosing for me" — quietly compounding the existing "the platform's hotel inventory may not be complete" perception drag.

2Three Rounds of Debate

Round 1 · Who Is This Really For?
value positioning
🔵 Xia Zhi (Pro · Light) · Opening

Honestly, every time I book a hotel it feels like sitting a reading-comprehension exam. Eighty 4-star options in a single city, with prices, locations, breakfast, refund policies, ratings shoved at me like aisles in a supermarket. It's not that I don't want to choose — I don't have the ability to choose. I don't know whether business district or scenic area is better, whether "deluxe king" actually has a window, whether a family with a kid should pick the executive floor. If the platform could just tell me on the booking page, "based on your trip (5-year-old / 3 nights / Disney visit), we recommend this room because of A, B, C," I'd hit book in a heartbeat. I don't need more options — I need someone to narrow it down to 1-2.

🟢 Lin Chuan (Con · Heavy) · Response

"Narrowing down" is exactly what I'm afraid of. My booking flow has only three steps: confirm this hotel, confirm executive room, confirm breakfast included. I know this hotel better than the AI does — I stayed here 3 times last month. An AI "recommendation" means the platform takes my own preferences, trains a model dumber than me, then plants it where I'd click the room I actually want. I'm not against AI — I'm against the word "automatic". Help Xia Zhi narrow her options, sure, but don't pay for it by collapsing the room types I'm familiar with. Both can coexist, but the default state must be "show everything, info crystal clear"; AI recommendations belong in an opt-in entry, not a forced top slot.

Round 2 · Trust and the Black Box
non-negotiable rules if AI ships
🔵 Xia Zhi

You said "the AI is dumber than me." From a light user's perspective, I'm dumber than the AI. I had to Google what "executive room" even means. So I welcome AI doing this for me. But your concern is valid — I also hate being recommended things I've already booked (a real pain point, not invented). My non-negotiables: One, give me the reason ("because last time you booked an executive floor with your kid"). Two, one-tap turnoff back to the full room list. Three, the recommended price cannot exceed the same room elsewhere — otherwise this is hidden advertising. If those three aren't met, I'd rather keep flipping through Xiaohongshu.

🟢 Lin Chuan

Your three rules — as a heavy user, I sign all of them. Add a fourth: the AI-recommended room, once chosen, must be remembered as my default next time, instead of being "personalized" all over again — because the essence of a heavy user is preference stability, not "endless exploration." Zooming out: the platform's bigger problem isn't a missing AI recommendation, it's that users still doubt whether the platform's hotel inventory is complete. Before that perception is fixed, handing the first-screen real estate to an AI still in training means trading a certain loss for an uncertain gain. Lock in "info density + full inventory + price transparency" first; put AI recommendations on the second screen as a "lazy lane." That sequence is safer.

Round 3 · Where and How Does It Land?
default state, entry, persona tiering
🔵 Xia Zhi

"Lazy lane on the second screen" — I oppose. Light users never scroll to the second screen. Our attention window is 3 seconds on the first screen. Hide the AI recommendation, and it might as well not exist. My version: a top row AI card occupying 1/4 of the first screen, with the full room list directly below. The card shows "this room + a one-line reason + price," followed by a "see all rooms" button. I'm satisfied, you're not disturbed — you scroll half a screen to your familiar list. The whole thing costs you 0.5 seconds but saves me 30 minutes.

🟢 Lin Chuan

0.5 seconds × 50 bookings/year = 25 seconds. Not a lot — but the real cost isn't time, it's my trust in this app. The moment I sense it's choosing for me, I instinctively wonder, "is it pocketing a kickback?" My counter-proposal: persona tiering based on bookings in the last 12 months. Heavy users get a "Power Mode" toggle (default: full room list + AI folded into a top icon). Light users get "Quick Mode" (your version). New users default to Quick Mode; after 3 bookings, prompt once: "you've become a fluent user — switch to Power Mode?" That way the AI recommendation is no longer the product's default form, it's a form the user opts into — neither side compromises.

🔵 Xia Zhi · Follow-up

I can accept "persona tiering," but a concern: many light users don't realize they're light, and reflexively pick "Power Mode" so they don't look dumb. So the toggle wording must avoid the "expert vs newbie" shame trap. Rename them "Full List" vs "Quick Recommend" — neutral feature names, no ability judgment.

🟢 Lin Chuan · Closing

Agreed.

3Moderator's Synthesis

✅ Consensus — Four Hard Rules

  1. AI recommendations must show their reasoning — no black box. The card must explain "why this room."
  2. AI recommendations must be one-tap dismissible, returning to the full room list with persistent memory.
  3. The recommended price cannot exceed the same room shown elsewhere — otherwise it's a hidden ad slot.
  4. AI recommendations must not collide with negative states like "already booked" or "out of stock" (no repeating the "already booked, still recommending" pain point).

🎯 Key Judgments for the Business

Judgment 1: Light users will likely score significantly higher PMF on AI recommendations than heavy users — but heavy users typically contribute a higher share of GMV (the 80/20 rule in hotel categories). This is not a feature that can be answered by a single PMF number. It must be evaluated through persona-tiered differentiation.
Judgment 2: The real risk isn't "is the AI accurate enough" — it's the moment heavy users sense the platform is deciding for them. That trust erosion is invisible in short-term metrics but accumulates as "the platform isn't professional / isn't comprehensive" — compounding the existing "the platform's hotel inventory may not be complete" perception drag, and pushing churn higher.
