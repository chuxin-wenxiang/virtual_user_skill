Key Findings & Experience Blind Spots

Why Option C Wins: Optimal Balance of Information Density + Low Cognitive Load

The comparison matrix delivers "everything visible on one screen" information density — users get a full picture of room-type differences without any additional interaction. The AI "Best for You" label further reduces decision cost by adding a one-click shortcut on top of comprehensive information display. This combination strongly appeals to both "rational comparison" users (white-collar professionals, established mid-income) and "efficiency-oriented" users, forming the broadest beneficiary base in this evaluation.

Option A — Strength & Limitation: Efficiency Shortcut for Repeat Users, Blank Page for New Ones

Option A achieved the highest score across all options in "Experience Clarity" (8.0), confirming that auto-recommendation is extremely intuitive — highlight + label, zero learning curve. However, its heavy dependency on historical data is a critical weakness: new/infrequent users (~40% of sample) rated Need-Fit significantly lower because "it can't recommend anything without history." Furthermore, frequent users acknowledged its convenience but rated "Potential Impact" low — they already know what they want.

Option B — Sharp Polarization: Strong Signal from Young Explorers vs. High Barrier for All

Option B's Experience Clarity SD of 1.82 (highest in the study) reveals severe divergence in how users perceive conversational interaction: Gen-Z and explorer-type users rated 7–8 (accustomed to ChatGPT paradigm), while efficiency-oriented and older users rated only 3–5 ("I still have to figure out what to ask it"). The "Potential Impact" score (7.4) indicates it genuinely changes decision behavior once adopted, but "Priority Urgency" is the lowest (4.8) — most users feel "hotel room selection isn't complex enough to need this."

Critical Experience Blind Spots

Option A: Cold-start problem — first-time platform users or cross-context bookings (switching from business to leisure) get inaccurate recommendations; requires a fallback design.

Option B: Interaction efficiency — "By the time I type out my requirements, I could have scrolled through all the room options myself" (efficiency-oriented users); needs quick-entry shortcuts or preset question templates.

Option C: Information overload risk — when a hotel has more than 6 room types, the matrix may become cluttered; needs collapse/filter mechanisms.

Delivery Priority Recommendation

P0 (First Release): Option C — Comparison Matrix + AI Label

Broadest user benefit, lowest opinion divergence (lowest SD across all dimensions), moderate development complexity (structured data + labeling logic), works immediately without historical user data (no cold-start issue). Recommended initial coverage: hotel detail pages with 3–6 room types.

P1 (Second Phase): Option A — History-Based Recommendation (as C Enhancement)

Layer A on top of C: for returning users with booking history, auto-highlight their preferred room type within the comparison matrix. This solves the cold-start problem (C provides the fallback) while preserving efficiency gains for repeat customers. Implementation path: ship C first, then overlay A's labeling logic within C.

P2 (Long-Term Exploration): Option B — Conversational AI Room Selector

Currently carries high user education cost and low perceived urgency. Recommend retaining as an "advanced feature" entry point, initially available as a gray-release to Gen-Z and first-time booking users. Can combine with C: conversation results rendered as a comparison matrix.

Experiment Design Recommendations

Experiment Type: A/B Test (random traffic split)

Minimum Sample: ≥ 2,000 unique users per arm (based on expected 5% conversion lift, α=0.05, power=0.8)

Observation Period: 14 days (covers weekend effects + return-visit cycles)

Stratification Dimensions: Historical booking frequency (high/low/new) × Device (iOS/Android) × Membership tier

Analysis Framework: Day 1–3 immediate conversion delta; Day 7 return-visit retention; Day 14 final report

Methodology Note

This evaluation is based on real user research scenario library (54,631 de-identified records). Vector retrieval matched scenarios related to "hotel booking / room selection / AI assistant / comparison." From the matched results, 20 diverse virtual user personas were generated. Each user independently evaluated all 3 options across 4 weighted dimensions (Need-Fit 30% + Experience Clarity 25% + Potential Impact 25% + Priority Urgency 20%), with final weighted aggregation for ranking. Scoring extrapolation logic is modeled on the match between user behavioral characteristics and solution properties, not random assignment. Sample composition: Gen-Z (25%), Established Mid-Income (35%), Young Professionals (20%), Family/Older (10%), Other (10%).
